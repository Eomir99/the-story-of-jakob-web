The pitch

I wrote my bachelor thesis on how video game companies adapt their products for international markets. Ten semi-structured interviews with people working in and around Nordic studios, analysed through the integration-responsiveness framework and localization theory. It was, in every practical sense, six months of market research on this industry.

What I found is that adaptation is rarely a redesign. Studios keep a globally standardised core and adapt selectively — localization, monetization, regional marketing, platform-specific implementation, regulatory compliance. And it doesn't stop at launch. Live-service titles keep adapting for years, driven by telemetry, community feedback and shifting regulation. Localization has stopped being a translation task and become a strategic capability.

Doing that research is what turned a lifelong interest in games into an interest in the business behind them. The questions I kept finding most interesting were the commercial ones — why a title is priced differently across regions, why a storefront requirement reshapes a roadmap, why some markets are institutionally harder to enter than others. Those are the questions a market research function exists to answer, and they are particularly live at a company running long-tail, community-driven titles across a lot of markets at once.

I am a recent graduate, and I won't pretend to industry experience I don't have. What I bring is a year spent studying this specific industry closely enough to have opinions worth arguing with, an exchange semester and consulting projects that taught me to turn messy input into a structured recommendation, and a habit of building the thing rather than describing it — which is roughly why you have just played a platformer instead of opening a PDF.

The thesis

The strategic importance of product adaptation in the video game industry

A case study based on Nordic videogame companies

Bachelor thesis, University of Gothenburg, Spring 2026. Written with Axel Abrahamsson. Supervisor: Niklas Åkerman.

Question: How do video game firms strategically approach and implement product adaptation within international markets?

Method: Qualitative, abductive. Ten semi-structured interviews with industry professionals connected to Nordic studios and the wider international industry, analysed through the integration-responsiveness framework and localization theory.

What it found:

  • Firms almost never redesign a game for a region. They hold a standardised core and adapt selectively — localization, monetization, regional marketing, platform implementation, limited content changes.
  • Localization increasingly operates as an integrated strategic capability rather than a post-production translation step.
  • Regulatory and institutional adaptation is a first-order concern: censorship, age ratings, data protection, platform policy, monetization rules. China is the most institutionally complex market studied.
  • Because games keep evolving after release, adaptation is a continuous operational process rather than a market-entry activity.
